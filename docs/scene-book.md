## **How to read a scene**

> * Narration is second person, present tense.  
> * **Bold** lines are the player's choices. The arrow (→) shows what happens.  
> * *Record* lines are what the game writes to the player's record. The record states facts. It never scores or judges.  
> * \[Brackets\] are placeholders Jim still needs to fill.

## **Opening: Monday (open world)**

*Starting life: functioning but drifting. The player's first in-game day. The town is open from the first minute. The main quest points a direction but never forces an order. The day ends at night, which is the chapter's natural stopping point.*

### **1\. Character creator**

* **Name.** Free text.  
* **Pronouns.** He/him, she/her, they/them.  
* **Look.** A flat-style portrait: skin tone, hair, build, clothes. Cosmetic only. Nothing here changes how the game plays.  
* **Starting life.** Functioning but drifting. Rock bottom and rebuilding after a loss show as "Coming later."

The 18+ check already ran before this screen. The creator ends on one line over the portrait: "Nothing's wrong. That's the problem." Then the game opens in your bedroom.

### **2\. The town (slice zone)**

The town is a map of places. Tap one to go there. Travel takes 10 to 20 in-game minutes. Time of day matters: places open and close, and people move around town.

* **Home.** Bedroom, kitchen, living room. Where mornings start and nights end.  
* **The job site.** Ray, the foreman. Your shift runs 7:00 to 3:30.  
* **The diner.** \[Marlene\] runs it. Regulars, coffee, and the news board where town news lives.  
* **The gym.** Mostly empty at 5 a.m. and 6 p.m. \[Deshawn\] owns it.  
* **The bar.** \[Tuck's\]. Opens at 4\. The bartender knows your order.  
* **The church.** The food pantry runs out of the basement. \[Pastor Ruth\] coordinates it.  
* **The park.** A walking track and a bench by the river.  
* **The gas station.** On \[Main\]. Where things break down, sometimes literally.

### **3\. Quest log on Monday morning**

**Main quest: Raise the Standard** (How to Rebuild Yourself, stage 1). Objective: "Get through Monday. Tonight, write one line you won't cross." The objective updates as the day goes.  
**Daily responsibility: Your shift.** 7:00 to 3:30 at the job site. The game records whether you show up, honestly. Skipping is possible.  
**Side quests you can find Monday:**

* **Saturday.** Dana's text. Answer it, or don't. (Ownership)  
* **Second Time This Week.** From Ray, only if you're late. (Ownership)  
* **The Cans by the Sink.** A small chore at home. The easiest floor version in the game.  
* **Coffee at the Diner.** \[Marlene\] has news, and the news board introduces how the town talks.  
* **Short Shelves.** The food pantry is short this week. Optional. It plants Purpose for later.

### **4\. Morning at home**

*Bedroom, 6:30 a.m. Dark blue room. The only light is the phone, face up on the nightstand.*  
The alarm goes off at 6:30. It has gone off at 6:30 for three years.  
The phone is already lit. Eleven notifications. You don't remember leaving it there, face up, a foot from your head. You never do.  
Nothing is wrong. That's the part nobody would notice. Rent is paid. The job is fine. You're fine. You've said "fine" so many times it doesn't sound like a word anymore.  
**Get up.** → Cold floor. You have time for coffee. *Record: Got up on the alarm.*  
**Check the phone. Just a minute.** (Escape: the phone) → It isn't a minute. It's forty. The relief is real: for forty minutes nothing asks anything of you. Then it's 7:10. *Record: 40 minutes on the phone before getting up.* The phone gains pull.  
**Snooze.** → Nine more minutes. Then nine more. You're up at 6:48 with less morning and no more rest. *Record: Snoozed twice.*  
From here, the house is yours to move through and the map is open. The kitchen holds two things: the cans by the sink (side quest), and Dana's text, which buzzes in the first time you check the phone or walk into the kitchen.  
Dana, your friend: "Still on for Saturday? Be honest if you're not. You bailed last time." You can answer now, later or never. The record keeps the time you answered.

* **"Work's been insane. I'll try."** → It works. A thumbs up. Nothing gets harder today. Nothing gets better either. *Record: Blamed work.*  
* **"I bailed. That's on me. I'll be there."** → "ok. holding you to that." A promise you can keep or break. *Record: Owned the miss. Promise made: Saturday with Dana.* Opens the Ownership skill line.  
* **Leave it on read.** → It sits in the quest log. It's still there at lunch, and at five. *Record: Didn't answer Dana.*

### **5\. Out in town**

The map marks the job site, because your shift starts at 7\. Nothing stops you from going somewhere else first. The diner is open. The gym is open. You'll be late, and the game won't pretend you weren't.  
**At the job site, if you're late:** Ray doesn't look up from his clipboard. "Second time this week."

* **"Traffic on \[Route 46\]."** → Ray writes something down. He's heard that one. *Record: Blamed traffic.*  
* **"That's on me. It won't be a pattern."** → Ray looks up for the first time. "Okay." That's all. It's more than you got from him last month. *Record: Owned being late. Promise made: on time.*

**If you're on time:** Ray says "Morning" without looking up. Nobody notices you were on time. In a small town, that's a kind of news too.  
The shift plays out in a few lines. You're good at the parts of this job nobody sees. At 3:30 the town opens back up: the diner, the gym, the park, the church, and at 4 o'clock, the bar.  
**At the bar:** The bartender sets your usual down before you ask. Nobody here judges you. That's the pull. The drinking escape lives here as a place, not just a card.

### **6\. The first encounter: "You earned it"**

It triggers around 9:40 p.m. wherever you are. On the couch at home by default. At the bar if you're still there. In the truck in a parking lot if you're out. The place changes the hand: the bar makes Couple beers stronger, and the truck makes Scroll stronger.  
Resolve and Pull show as numbers on screen. Resolve starts higher if you got up on the alarm, owned a miss today or did the cans. Pull starts higher if you were on the phone this morning or spent the evening at the bar.  
**The old voice opens:** "Long day. You earned this. Start tomorrow."  
**Escape cards (always in hand):**

* **Scroll.** Big relief now. Costs sleep. The phone gains pull.  
* **Couple beers.** Big relief now. Costs tomorrow morning, and two cans by the sink become four.  
* **Order in and zone out.** Some relief now. The cost is slow, and you won't see it tonight.

**Action cards (earned today):**

* **Name it.** Earned by owning a miss today. "This isn't rest. This is hiding." Weakens the voice's next argument.  
* **Phone in the kitchen.** Offered on the first night at home. Takes Scroll out of your hand tonight.  
* **The floor version.** Ten minutes of the thing you've been putting off: the cans, a walk to the park, or answering Dana.  
* **What am I waiting for?** Makes the old voice give its reason. If it has none, Resolve goes up.

**The old voice pushes back (samples):**

* Against Name it: "It's not hiding. It's one night."  
* Against the floor version: "Ten minutes won't change anything." Your answer: "It won't change everything. It's ten minutes I didn't skip."  
* Third round, if it gets there: "Start Monday. Fresh week." (The readiness trap.)

**How it can end. No defeat screen. Night falls either way.**

* **Acted.** You did the floor version. *Record: Did ten minutes of dishes at 9:52 p.m.* Resolve carries into tomorrow.  
* **Delayed.** "Tomorrow," with no new reason. *Record: Put it off to tomorrow. Nothing changed.*  
* **Escaped.** You played an escape card. The game tells the truth: it felt good. Then the cost lands overnight and in the morning, and tomorrow opens with the restart sequence. It's a way back, not a punishment. *Record: 11:40 p.m. Still scrolling.*  
* **Adjusted.** Only if a real new fact shows up. At 9:45, Dana calls: her car died at the gas station on \[Main\]. Going to her is a real change of plan, and it puts you on the map at night. *Record: Changed the plan. Helped Dana. The facts changed.*

### **7\. The first standard**

*Bedroom, before sleep. Where the phone sits depends on what you chose.*  
Before you sleep, one question. Not from anyone else. From you.  
**"What's one line you won't cross anymore?"**  
The player types it. The game won't accept a line that can't be kept or broken. It never scores the line.

* "Do better." → "Better than what? You can't break this, so you can't keep it either. Make it smaller and make it real."  
* "Never touch my phone again." → "You'll break this by 7 a.m. and quit by 7:05. One line that stops the bleeding. Not a new life."

Starters, built from what happened today: "The phone sleeps in the kitchen." "I answer Dana the same day." "I'm on site by 7."  
When the line is written: "Written. You can break this. That's what makes it real." The main quest updates. *Tag:* How to Rebuild Yourself, stage 1: Raise the standard. \[Learn it on Only Empowerment →\]  
"That's Monday. The town keeps going. Pick it up when you're ready."

## **Slice outline: stages 2 and 3 (draft 1\)**

*Covers the in-game days after Monday, about 8 to 10 days in all. These are quest outlines with key lines, not full scripts. Once Jim approves the outline, each quest gets drafted as a full scene, one at a time, the same way as the opening.*

### **The cast**

* **Dana.** Your friend. Owns the Saturday quest. She won't chase you. If you bail again, she says "ok" and stops asking, and her trust comes back on her schedule, not yours.  
* **Ray.** The foreman. Cares about one thing: whether you show up. As your record builds, he trusts you with more, starting with Thursday overtime.  
* **\[Marlene\].** Runs the diner and knows everyone. Her news board is how private choices go public in a small town.  
* **\[Deshawn\].** Owns the gym. Former athlete. He'll hand you a six-day program if you ask for one, and respect you more if you ask for the smallest version instead.  
* **\[Pastor Ruth\].** Runs the food pantry. Needs one volunteer hour on Thursdays. She asks once and never preaches.  
* **\[Gus\].** Bartender at \[Tuck's\]. Kind, no judgment, knows your order. He isn't a villain. The pull is how easy he makes it.  
* **\[Cole\].** A coworker on Ray's crew. Funny, generous, drifting the same way you are. He invites you out. He isn't the problem either.  
* **The old voice.** Not a person. It shows up in encounters and learns to argue in the terms of whichever escape you lean on.

### **Stage 2 quests: Build the Structure**

**Main quest: Build the Structure** (How to Rebuild Yourself, stage 2). Objective: "Set up your days so Monday's line can survive Tuesday." The stage ends after you've run your structure on any three days. They don't have to be in a row. The game counts returns, not streaks.  
**Main quest steps:**

* **Morning Line.** Pick your morning structure: where the alarm sits, where the phone sleeps, coffee or not. An overbuilt option is on the menu: 5 a.m. gym, cold shower, journaling, meal prep. It looks great on day one and collapses by day two. When it does, the restart sequence opens.  
* **The Kitchen Counter.** Your environment. The cans by the sink. What's in the fridge. Taking the beer out of the house is an option, and it earns the Remove the access card. This is where The Disciplined Operator's idea first shows up, without being named.  
* **The Daily Minimum.** Set a floor and a ceiling for one thing. Example: walk 10 minutes as the floor, 40 at the gym as the ceiling. On bad days the floor is what counts.  
* **Three Days.** Run it. Any three days. Misses go on the record as they happened, and each miss opens the restart sequence the next morning.

**Side quests:**

* **Saturday.** Only if you promised Dana on Monday. Saturday morning, the old voice opens with "You're tired. She'll understand." Keep it or break it. Either way, the record says which.  
* **Deshawn's Plan.** He offers the six-day program. Taking it is the overbuilding trap. Asking for the floor version earns his respect and a real routine.  
* **Cole's Thursday.** Cole invites you to \[Tuck's\] after the shift. Going isn't wrong. The test is which card you play once you're there.  
* **Short Shelves.** Continues from Monday. \[Pastor Ruth\] needs one hour on Thursday. It feeds your Purpose choice.

**The Purpose moment.** At the end of stage 2, the game asks: "What purpose must guide me now?" The choices come from what you did, for example: being someone Dana can count on, getting your body back, being worth trusting at work, or your own words. This opens the Purpose skill line.

### **Stage 3 quests: Repeat the Action**

**Main quest: Ordinary Days** (How to Rebuild Yourself, stage 3). Objective: "Keep the structure through a week where nothing special happens." The drama fades on purpose. As routines form, they collapse into one tap, like "Run my morning," so ordinary days play fast.  
**Main quest steps:**

* **The Boring Middle.** Days 4 to 6\. The newness is gone. The old voice: "This isn't working. Nothing's changed." You answer with the Show the record card.  
* **A Real New Fact.** Ray asks you to stay late Thursday, and it collides with something you planned. The game asks which this is: a plan change or a miss. It knows whether the facts actually changed.  
* **Rain Day.** You wake up sick, or the weather kills the plan. Only the floor version is possible. This plants the question "What can I still do?" The Resilience skill line itself opens in Phase 2\.  
* **The Week Back.** End of the stage. The record shows the week as it happened, with returns counted and misses shown. Your first trait can form here, such as "someone who shows up" from your shift record or "someone who answers" from Dana's texts.

**Side quest: Marlene's News.** The news board mentions you, for better or worse: seen at \[Tuck's\] at 11, or seen at the pantry Thursday. Private choices showing up in public is the Integrity rule at work.  
The slice ends after The Week Back: "That's the first stretch. The rest of town is coming."

### **Starting encounter cards**

**Escape cards (always in hand):** Scroll, Couple beers, and Order in and zone out, the same as the opening. Place changes their strength.  
**Action cards you can earn in the slice:**

* **Name it.** Earned by owning a miss. Weakens the voice's next argument. (Ownership)  
* **Phone in the kitchen.** Earned in Morning Line. Takes Scroll out of your hand at home. (Structure)  
* **Remove the access.** Earned in The Kitchen Counter. Takes Couple beers out of your hand at home. (Structure)  
* **The floor version.** Earned in The Daily Minimum. Ten minutes of the real thing beats none of it. (Structure)  
* **What am I waiting for?** Makes the old voice give its reason. If it has none, Resolve goes up. (Pure Execution Mode)  
* **Show the record.** Earned after any three days of structure. Answers "Nothing's changed." (Proof)  
* **Text Dana first.** Earned if Dana's trust is high enough. Real support, not rescue. (Support)  
* **It's a plan change.** Playable only when a real new fact exists. Using it without one shows up later in Integrity.  
* **Remember why.** Earned after the Purpose moment. Its strength grows with your Purpose skill line. (Purpose)

**The old voice's arguments, by break point:**

* Waiting for readiness: "Start Monday. Fresh week."  
* Overbuilding: "If you're doing this, do it right. All of it."  
* No daily minimum: "No time for the full thing, so skip it."  
* Negotiating with the standard: "Just this once. You've been good."  
* Chaos: "Everything's falling apart anyway."  
* The boring middle: "Nothing's changed. Why bother?"
